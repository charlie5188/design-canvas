# Shell test checklist

Run this after any change to `shell/`. Use a canvas with at least one plain
item, one `viewport` item, one item with a nested scroll area, and one with a
menu or popover. Only mark a line as passed if you ran it; say which lines need
a device or input you do not have.

## Camera

- On the canvas page with the default tool, a drag on empty space draws a selection box (see Selection) and does not pan; a wheel or two-finger scroll pans. A drag on empty space pans in a shared file, by touch, and with Ask agent or Inspect chosen. Space + drag and middle-button drag pan anywhere, including over previews and `viewport` frames (middle-button over a frame is a known limit).
- ⌘/Ctrl + wheel and trackpad pinch zoom around the pointer, over empty space, previews, and frames, in both directions without jumps or drift.
- A canvas opened in a background or zero-size tab fits all items once it has a real size, never at minimum zoom.
- After a reload the canvas is at its saved pan and zoom from the first frame. It does not show at the default view and then jump.
- Toolbar zoom buttons and `+`, `-` step between fixed levels (10, 25, 50, 100, 200, 300%) around the viewport center: from 100% a press goes to 200% or 50%, from a level between two (94% after a fit) to the next one in that direction, and the ends hold. Wheel and pinch stay continuous. `0` resets the camera only. `1` and "show all" fit every item, including titles and handles. These keys work with the canvas, a note, a section's tab, or the toolbar focused, never while typing in a field or with a preview focused.
- Figma's view keys: Shift + 1 fits all, Shift + 0 zooms to 100% around the viewport center, Shift + 2 fits the selected note or section (its title included) and does nothing with nothing selected.
- Holding Z (not in a text field) turns the cursor into a zoom-in cursor everywhere, over previews and `viewport` frames too, and Z + ⌥/Alt into zoom-out. Z + click zooms one level in around the pointer, Z + Alt + click one level out; Z + drag draws a box and zooms to fit it, up to 300%. None of these clicks a control, copies a reference, places an annotation, moves anything, or changes the selection, also in annotate mode and with a Note or Section tool armed. Z typed in a preview's field or a note types the letter. With focus inside a `viewport` frame, Z still arms the zoom. Releasing Z, or leaving the window, restores the cursor.
- Wheel over a nested scroll area scrolls it until its end, then pans. The same inside a frame.
- Horizontal trackpad swipes never trigger browser Back or Forward.
- Arrow keys pan when the canvas has focus. Tabbing to an offscreen control moves the camera to show it.

## Cursors

- An item's grip and name show an open hand; a pan or an item move shows a closed hand everywhere, including over previews and frames. Holding Space shows the open hand everywhere. Empty space shows the plain arrow where a drag there selects (the canvas page, default tool), also while the box is drawn, and the open hand where a drag there pans (a shared file, Ask agent, Inspect, the Hand tool).
- Previews keep the app's own cursors: a text cursor over text, the app's pointers on controls.
- Holding Alt/Option over a preview or a frame shows the copy cursor.
- In annotate mode previews, frames, and item titles show an arrow with a see-through speech bubble at its lower right; the arrow's tip is the point a click asks about. With the Note tool the pointer is an arrow with a see-through sticky note, everywhere on the canvas; the Section tool keeps the crosshair. What is under either shape stays readable. A pan or Space still shows the hand, and Z the zoom cursor. Not verified on a high-density display or in Safari and Firefox, where the pointer may be drawn at another size.

## Items

- Dragging the grip or the name moves an item at every zoom level, with mouse and touch; arrow keys on the focused grip move it, Shift for larger steps. Moving never remounts a preview or clears its input.
- A dragged item snaps: when an edge or the centre of its preview comes within about 6 px on screen (at any zoom) of an edge or centre of another item's preview, it catches there, and a 1 px guide line runs through the previews that share the line. Previews align, not whole items, so an item with a caption still lines up with one without. Moving clear of the line releases it and hides the guide; the guides are gone once the drag ends, and the aligned position is what is saved. Holding Shift keeps the drag on the axis it has travelled furthest along. Items do not snap to notes or sections, and resizing does not snap. Not verified by touch.
- An item resizes from its border like an OS window: over any edge or corner the cursor turns into a resize cursor (nothing else changes), and dragging resizes it at every zoom level. The grab zone is about 10 px on screen at any zoom. The top zone and the top corners never reach up past the gap between the name row and the preview: at 25% and below, the name, the caption, and the actions (Pick, Ask agent, More) take a press, and the top edge still resizes from just inside the preview. The opposite side stays put, a north or west drag also moves the item, and a `viewport` item's layout follows its width as it is dragged. The new width, height, and position land in `registry.tsx` once the person pauses, and ⌘/Ctrl + Z puts them back. An entry that takes its size from a spread (`...SIZE`) gets the new size written after the spread, and keeps it after a reload. Not while annotating or in a shared file. Not verified by keyboard or touch.
- While an item or a section is resized, a small label just below and to the right of the pointer shows its size in canvas pixels, at any zoom: both as "W × H" from a corner, and only the one that changes from an edge ("W 480" or "H 320"). It flips to the other side near the window's edge and is gone on release. A note shows none.
- An item's name is a title. A click on it copies nothing and shows nothing; dragging it moves the item. It is on one line: a name too long for the item, or for the room the actions leave while they show, is cut with an ellipsis, and its whole text shows on hover. The preview never moves down when the actions or Restart appear.
- An item's actions sit at the frame's top right, level with the name, and do not move with the name's length. They are quiet until the item is hovered (its name or its preview) or one has keyboard focus; on a touch screen they always show. In order: Restart (only once the item was used), Ask agent about the item, Pick (the only one with a word), More. A press on an action never starts a move.
- Ask agent about the item opens a note on the whole item with no tool chosen first, the same note a click on the name makes in Ask agent mode. A new, empty note about a whole item offers four quick requests (Flow, Phone, States, Languages): a press puts the words in the field, which keeps the focus, and the requests go once there are words. A second press on the button opens the note that waits; it does not add another. Not in a shared file.
- More opens on a press and holds "Open in new tab" (not where the canvas has no address of its own) and "Move to trash". Escape, a press outside it, or a choice closes it.
- The open and trash icons appear when the title is hovered or the icon has keyboard focus, and are always visible on touch devices. The open icon opens `frame.html?item=<id>` in a new tab without changing the canvas. Pressing either never starts a move.
- The check beside an item's name (shown with the open and trash icons) picks it. In one step the item gets a "Picked" tag and a ring around its preview, and stays where it is. Every item tag sits just under the bottom-right corner of the item's preview, close to the card and clear of its name and caption at any caption length, and one tag shows there at a time, in this order: "Agent working"; "Sent", "Queued", or "Not applied yet"; "New" or "Updated"; an overdue "Saving…"; "Picked". "Picked" is drawn as a tab hanging from the ring's bottom edge, in the ring's color, just inside the rounded corner; the others are pills a few pixels below the card. The ring shows whatever the tag says. A tag lets presses through, so the card still resizes from the edge under it; only a tag's own button takes a press. Notes and sections keep their tag above their top-right corner. Then the other items of its group move into one "Set aside" section below everything else, in reading order, each whole inside the frame; a notice says how many were set aside and offers Undo. The group is the item's section, or the items in no section: an item in another section never moves. When sections sit inside a section, the group is the outermost one: the sections inside it, except the picked item's own, move into "Set aside" whole, each with its items, notes, and inner sections shifted by the same amount and its title tab inside the frame; the other items of the picked item's own section move one by one; the picked item's section and the outer section stay. The camera does not move. The registry gets `picked: true`, the section with the id `set-aside`, and the moved items' `x` and `y` in one write.
- Undo from the notice and ⌘/Ctrl + Z are the same undo: either puts the registry back exactly (the mark, the section, every position), also when the pick was made within two seconds of another undo. Picking a new version later sets the earlier pick aside too: it loses its mark and joins the right end of the same section, which grows; there is still one section.
- Hovering a picked item shows an × on its "Picked" tab (always shown on a touch screen; never in a shared file or while annotating). Right after a pick, with nothing saved since, it is the same as the pick's Undo: the mark, the "Set aside" section, and every position go back. Later, it removes only the mark (`picked: false` in the registry), the set-aside items stay where they are, and a notice "Unpicked <name>" offers Undo, which puts the mark back. ⌘/Ctrl + Z undoes it too.
- The check is not shown on the picked item, on an item inside a set-aside section (id `set-aside` or starting with `set-aside-`), while annotating, or in a shared file. The "Picked" tag and ring do show in a shared file. When the write cannot be made (no dev server), nothing is left half done: the items are back where they were and a notice says so.
- A pan or move that ends over a control does not click it.
- Previews keep clicking, typing, text selection, focus, and their own keyboard shortcuts. Space inside an input types a space.
- Menus, popovers, and dialogs open in the right place at 50%, 100%, and 200% zoom.
- Touching or focusing an item brings it above overlapping items, so a covered handle becomes reachable; only one item is in front.
- A planned direction (`component: planned({ … })` from `Planned.tsx`) shows its sketch, its bet, what it gives up, and its reference in the canvas's own colors and font, whatever the app's stylesheet does, and fills its item. The sketch is a white screen at most 460 px wide, with the words beside it on a wide card and below it on a narrow one. In the sketch, a `Block` with a `size` is at least that size and never cuts off its label; boxes without one share the rest, and no box is shorter than what it holds. A box with a label draws its outline; one without only arranges. Each `kind` draws a shape people recognize, with its words: heading, text, button (filled when `primary`), field, choice (filled dot when `checked`), toggle (the `selected` segment raised), list, table (column names, then rows), and media (a crossed box). In a row, fields share the width and controls keep their own height. A box with `emphasis` has an accent outline and one "Key change" tag; a marked box inside it adds no second tag. Sketches written with the older boxes only (labels and lines) still read as before. It moves, resizes, goes to the trash and comes back, takes annotations, and has its text rewritten in place like any item, and each of those is saved into its registry entry, sketch included.
- On the canvas page a planned card has two buttons. **Drop** moves it to the trash with the usual Undo. **Build this** adds a sent note on the whole card and sends it at once with anything else waiting: with a listening agent the card's item shows "Agent working" and the card says it asked; with no agent the note is copied. The message has no new-version line. Once the agent replaces the card's component, the pin clears. The buttons do not show in the shared file, on an item's own page, in a `viewport` frame, or in the trash's previews. A press in annotate mode annotates the button and does not press it.
- An item that the person has used gets a Restart action, last among the actions beside its name. It shows only once the item's content, or what a field in it holds, differs from what it was before the person first touched it; it goes again when the content is back as it was. Restart mounts that item again, a plain item's component and a `viewport` item's frame, and no other item, with no page reload. A click in Ask agent mode, a rewritten text, a move, and a resize do not show it. It also works in a shared file.

## Navigation

- A link in a preview, in a portaled menu, or inside a frame leaves the URL unchanged and still runs the component's own click handler. A router stand-in's call goes the same way as a link.
- An item stands for a browser tab, and a link or a router call stays in the item it is in. The item names its paths (`path`, and `screens` by path; `[id]` or `:id` is any one segment; the query and hash are ignored). A path that is one of the item's screens shows in place, in a plain item and in a `viewport` frame, and no other item changes. Any other path shows a notice ("This item has no screen for …", or "Links stay on the canvas: …" in an item that names no paths), and the view does not move, also when another item shows that path. A router call does not say which item it came from, so the item last touched stands for it.
- Only a link that opens a new tab in the product (`target="_blank"`) leaves its item, and it never changes the item it is in, also when the path is one of that item's own screens. The camera glides, in about a quarter of a second, to the nearest other item that shows the path and puts it in the middle of the view, also when it was in view already, keeping the zoom unless the item needs more room; that item comes to the front, shows a ring for a moment, and shows that screen. A press, a wheel turn, or a key during the glide stops it where it is, and the person goes on from there; with reduced motion the camera is set at once. With no such item, a notice says "Opens in a new tab: …".
- An item with more than one screen shows the number of screens, with a route icon, beside its name, always; an item with one screen shows none. A press on it opens the flow view over the whole canvas: the item's screens in registry order, each live at the item's size, named by path; it opens with the whole flow fitted to the window, then pans and zooms as the canvas does (drag, also on a screen; wheel; Ctrl or ⌘ + wheel or a pinch at the pointer; + and −; 1 to fit; 0 for real size; the zoom bar at the bottom right, the same as the canvas's: −, level, +, fit all, actual size), from 10% to 200%. At the top left, a bar like the canvas's toolbars: back to the canvas, the item's name and number of screens, and Ask agent about the whole flow. Each screen has a name row like an item's: its path, and Ask agent at the right while the screen is hovered. A drag never opens a screen. Screens, their names, and the words on arrows scale with the view; the words read at 12px at the zoom the view opens at. Lines, borders, and pins keep one size on screen at any zoom. the screen the item shows now ringed. The main path runs left to right in registry order. A screen that its previous one does not lead to, but an earlier one does, is a branch: it sits in a row under the screen it branches from, and the screens after it on the branch go on to its right; a screen nothing leads to yet stays on the main path. The layout is made as the view opens and holds still while it is open. Arrows join screens: a short one to the next screen in a row, one down into a branch and one back up out of it, and a loop for any other step, each loop in its own lane with the shorter loops inside, so none cross: under the row when there is one row, over the main path when there are branches. Each arrow says what makes the step in the control's own words, as click "Pay": a link's text, or for a router call the control pressed just before it; a control with no words gives an arrow with none. A long label takes two lines between two screens; on a loop the line stops around the words. Links read from each screen give arrows; a new-tab link gives none; a step made by a router call gets one once the person made it in the item. The screens take no clicks. A press on a screen closes the view and shows the item at that screen, centred; Escape, the close button, or a press on the empty space closes it with nothing changed. `viewport` items show their screens as frames, and their links give arrows too. Works in a shared file. Under reduced motion it appears at once.
- States belong to a screen (`states` in the registry). The canvas shows each item in its first state, and the button by the name counts screens (route icon, when more than one) and states (stack icon); an item with one screen and states shows it too. In the flow view a screen with states has a pressed group of chips beside its name, Default first; a press shows that state in place, in a frame too, and nothing moves. A note asked on a state is named `Name › screen /path (State)`, and its pin shows while that state does. The view opens on the first states.
- In the flow view, the tools bar at the bottom centre has Interact and Ask agent, as on the canvas, and A and V choose them. With Ask agent, the element under the pointer in a screen is outlined; a click on it opens a note there, named `Name › screen /path › button “Pay”`, and the screen's own control does nothing and takes no focus; a click on a frame item's screen, or on a screen's frame, asks about the whole screen. Escape puts the tool down before it closes the view. A note on an element shows as a pin at its point in the screen, and on the canvas at that point on the item. Ask agent also sits in a screen's name row while it is hovered, and for the whole flow in the top left bar. Typing in a note opened under that bar types; the canvas's letter keys (A, N, S, V) never act while the flow view is open. Either opens a note right there, in the view: on the screen asked about, with its pin inside the screen's top left corner, or under the bar's button for the whole flow. Nothing behind the view moves, and the item does not change screen. Escape or a press on empty space closes the note first, then a second one closes the view. The note belongs to the item: it shows on the item once the view closes, and is named `Name › screen /path` or `Name › the whole flow (/a → /b → /c)`, and goes to the agent like any other. A note about the same screen that still waits opens again. Neither shows in a shared file.
- A router stand-in's `back()` returns the item to the screen it showed before, and its `usePathname()` gives the path of the screen shown. Restart returns the item to its first screen, and is offered on an item that a link in another item brought to a later screen, though the person did not touch it. A reload starts every item at its first screen.
- Start here: once an item shows Restart, its More menu lists Start here first; at the item's start it does not. It opens a note on the whole item, "Apply to a new version" unchecked, that names the screen shown (with its query) and the steps since the start: a pressed link or button (also one whose screen is gone by the time the press is heard), the last value typed in each field, a chosen option, a checked box, Enter or Escape that changed something. A click that changed nothing, and the click that focuses a field, are not steps. Restart empties the list, and so does a `viewport` frame that loads again. In a `viewport` item it names the frame's own screen and steps. Pressed again while its note waits, it updates that note instead of adding one. No Start here in a shared file.
- An item with `start` opens at that screen on the canvas, after a reload, on its own page, and in a `viewport` frame; Restart returns there, and is not offered at the start. The flow view still lays out every screen from the first, with the start screen marked as the one shown. A link inside the item resolves a relative path or a query against the start.
- On an item's own page (`frame.html?item=<id>`), for a `viewport` item and a plain one, links, router calls, `back()`, and forms follow the same rules: a path that is one of the item's screens shows in place, and anything else shows the canvas's notice at the top centre of the page. Neither the page nor the item's frame loads another address, and no tab opens. A reload starts the item at its first screen.
- A link to `#<item-id>` moves the camera to that item and brings it to the front, zooming out only as far as it needs.
- A native form submit is cancelled with a notice; a form that cancels its own submit shows nothing.
- A pan or move that ends on a link shows no notice.
- A link or router call with a query (`/contracts?status=signed`, or `?status=signed` alone) keeps it on the item: the path decides the screen, `useSearchParams()` in the stand-in reads the query, and Back returns to the screen before with its query. `useParams()` gives the values the screen's `[param]` parts matched. Restart clears both. The same holds inside a `viewport` frame.
- In a React Router app (the installer sets `ScreenRouter` to `canvas/stand-ins/react-router.tsx` in providers.tsx), an app's page component used as an item works as in the app: `NavLink` marks the current path, `setSearchParams` filters in place, a `<Link>` to `/contracts/:id` shows that screen with `useParams()` set, `navigate(-1)` goes back with the filter kept, and a link or `navigate()` to a path the item does not have shows a notice. The same holds inside a `viewport` frame. An app without React Router never loads the stand-in, and its canvas still type-checks.
- Updating a canvas installed before the stand-ins moved into the shell (`canvas/stand-ins/`): its old copies in `mocks/` are no longer loaded (the page requests `canvas/stand-ins/…`), unless a copy exports more than the shell's, which stays; a server action works as a stand-in without any change to its vite config; and `update.sh` lists the copies to delete, the `providers.tsx` line for React Router, and, once the app leaves the canvas folder out of its own checks, the canvas's own `exclude`. A fresh install has no stand-ins in `mocks/`, and a Next.js canvas's shared file builds and opens from disk with its stand-ins.

## Toolbar

- Controls sit in three places: tools (Interact, Move canvas, Inspect when a design system is set, Ask agent, Note, Section) at the bottom centre; zoom out, zoom level, zoom in, show all, and reset view at the bottom right; and the file's own at the top right: theme, then the trash (only while it holds items, with its count) and share, then Canvas settings and help. The trash comes and goes at the left of its group, so nothing under the pointer moves; it and the share panel open under the top bar, above the Inspect panel. All are icon buttons with tooltips that open away from the screen edge and stay on screen. The bottom holds only controls. What the canvas tells the person is at the top centre: a bar that appears only while a change is waiting (Discard and, while any change is unsent, Copy, or Apply while an agent listens), and under it a notice for a few seconds ("Copied 1 for the agent", "Moved … to the trash · Undo"); with no bar, the notice takes its place. A notice never hides the bar: "1 isn't sent yet" shows under the Copy it means. Nothing shifts under the pointer when either appears or goes. The help list opens on hover or keyboard focus, below the top bar, and describes the canvas region for screen readers.
- Narrow windows (375px): the view bar stacks above the tools bar, the changes bar and notices drop under the top bar, and no bar covers another. Show all and revealed items stop short of the top bars and the bottom bars; a revealed item also stops below the row a notice takes, so a notice never covers a new version as it arrives.
- At narrow widths (375 px) the text labels and separators drop and every control stays on screen, with annotations and trashed items present.

- The help button opens a panel of the canvas's controls on a press, not on hover: four groups (Move around, Items, Ask the agent, Notes and sections), each row with what it does and how, and each key drawn as a key cap. It stays open while the person tries something, and closes on Escape, a press outside it, or the button again. It fits in the window, in one column on a narrow one. A shared file's help leaves out the agent's group, the notes' group, and Undo.

## Look and motion

- Every icon comes from one set (`icons.tsx`, Tabler's drawings) and has one stroke weight on screen, 1.5 px, at every size. No icon is drawn by hand beside them.
- Controls and surfaces share one scale: 6 px corners on small controls and hints, 8 px on buttons and fields, 12 px on bars, popovers, and the note for the agent; one shadow for bars and notices, and one for what opens over the work.
- An open note for the agent is above its item's tag: "Picked" is never drawn over it.
- A control's wash and color ease in on hover, and a button gives a little under a press. A popover, a notice, a tag, a hint, and a pin fade in as they appear; a hint waits a moment first. Every surface that opens over the work (the item's More menu, Share, Help, Canvas settings, the Inspect panel and its option lists, the trash) also rises a few pixels, all the same way. Nothing animates out.
- A note placed with the Note tool sticks on, in under half a second: it drops in tilted with a lifted shadow, lands with a small squash, its outline fades in, and its corner folds down last. Typing works from the first frame. It plays once, when placed: not for a draft back after a reload, a saved note, or a note being edited. Off under reduced motion.
- When a pick sets the others aside, and when Undo brings items back, the items glide to their new place. An item never glides during a drag, a resize, or a hot update, and never trails the pointer. A note for the agent that is dragged aside follows the pointer from the first frame, also straight after it opens.
- With "reduce motion" on, none of this moves.
- The chrome's text is drawn antialiased; the text inside a preview is drawn as the app draws it.

## Themes

- With a theme store, the toolbar shows one pressed button per theme; without one, it shows none.
- Switching applies the theme to the canvas, its chrome, and every `viewport` frame, and survives reload.
- A frame page opened alone uses the saved theme.

## Background

- The gear at the top right (Canvas settings) opens the canvas's settings: under Background, Default, White, Dark, with the current one in bold. A choice closes the list; Escape or a press outside closes it too. It is there in a shared file as well.
- White and Dark change the board and all chrome on it (names, captions, section tabs, item tags, bars, popovers, notes) to colors that read on it, whatever the project's theme. What a preview shows (also in the trash and the flow view) keeps the project's colors. Default goes back to the project's theme.
- On any background, text in a preview with no color of its own has the app's `body` color, not the chrome's: on the canvas, in the trash, in the flow view, in a `viewport` frame, opened alone, and in a shared file. It follows a theme switch.
- The choice is kept per canvas in this browser and survives reload; Default keeps nothing. It is never written to the registry.

## References

- Holding Alt/Option outlines the element under the pointer in plain items and in frames; releasing hides it.
- Alt/Option-click copies `Item › role “label”`, never activates the control, and numbers look-alikes.
- Alt-click on empty canvas or on chrome does nothing.

## Annotations

- The Ask agent button and A (with the canvas, toolbar, or a pin focused) toggle annotate mode; Escape leaves it. A typed inside a preview or a field reaches that field.
- In annotate mode, hovering outlines elements in plain items and in frames without holding Alt; the outline hides when the camera moves.
- Clicking an element in a plain item or in a frame places a numbered pin at the click, also right after a zoom, and opens a note showing `Item › role “label”`. The control is not activated, and a link shows no notice and leaves the URL unchanged.
- Dragging across a plain item or a frame draws a box, kept inside the item, and on release opens a note showing `Item › area W×H at X,Y: …` with the controls, headings, and texts entirely inside it, in item pixels at any zoom. The area keeps its outline, with its pin at the top-left corner; no element note opens too. A press that barely moves stays a click on one element.
- Clicking an item's name or grip annotates the whole item (`Item`), outlined around the preview; dragging them still moves the item. Hovering a title outlines the whole preview.
- Double-clicking a text in a plain item or a frame edits its words in place: outlined, with a text cursor, the words selected, and no note open (the first click's empty note goes). Typing changes the words in the preview, so long copy wraps and pushes the layout as it would. The preview's own handlers, links, and the canvas shortcuts never see the keys or clicks; Shift + Enter breaks the line; an IME's Enter only confirms the composition. Enter, or a press anywhere else (also outside a frame), ends the edit: the new words stay where they are, with a dashed underline, and a note opens with `Change the text from “old” to “new”` (or `Remove the text “old”` when emptied) on the element's reference, which Enter saves and Escape removes. Escape during the edit cancels it and stays in annotate mode; an unchanged edit opens nothing. Several rewritten texts stay on show together, also outside annotate mode. Double-clicking new words edits them again, from the new words: the same note changes (its sentence is rewritten and anything the person added stays), and going back to the old words removes the note. The old words come back when the note goes: deleted, emptied, Escape on a new note, Discard, or cleared once applied (in place: the item renders the agent's words; new version: the original shows its old words). The agent's change to those words takes over from the preview, and a sent in-place note then clears. After a reload only the note is left. A label's field, an icon, or other elements among the words stay in place. After an edit, the item still updates its own text (for example a frame's width readout). A double-click on a field, an image, or empty space edits nothing.
- In annotate mode, clicking a section's title annotates the whole section (`Section “Title”`): hovering the title outlines the section, the pin sits just right of its title tab, above the items, at any zoom, the note has no "Apply to a new version" choice, and the section is not selected. Dragging the title still moves the section, and the pin moves with it. The message lists the items whose centre is inside it when sent, in reading order (rows top to bottom, each left to right), and the notes inside. A sent section note clears once an item is added or removed; the person moving things does not clear it. A new section the registry does not have yet cannot be annotated.
- The note field grows with its text, up to about ten lines, then scrolls; a long note (such as a rewritten text) opens showing all of it, on the side of the pin with room. Deleting text shrinks it back to three lines.
- Enter saves, Shift+Enter adds a line, and Enter that confirms an IME composition (Japanese, Chinese) does neither. Escape, in the note or on the checkbox, removes a new annotation and restores an edited one. Saving or cancelling returns focus to the pin.
- "Apply to a new version" is checked on an item's first annotation; later ones on that item start with the item's current choice. Changing it changes every unsent annotation on that item, and Escape undoes that too. Changing it on a sent annotation makes it unsent.
- Clicking elsewhere, or another element, keeps the typed text; an empty annotation is removed. Clicking a pin reopens it with the caret at the end; delete removes it.
- A note opened near the right or top edge of the window opens on the other side of its pin, so opening or typing in a note never pans the canvas.
- Space + drag and middle-button drag still pan over previews in annotate mode, and the pan places no annotation.
- Pins move with their item, keep one size from 10% to 300%, and follow the theme.
- Copy N for agent writes one message with the unsent annotations, numbered like their pins, and shows "Copied N for the agent". When any are checked, the last line reads "Keep the original and add a new version:" with those items and their numbers; with none checked there is no last line. Those pins turn into sent pins (check mark) and the next copy leaves them out. When the clipboard fails, the whole list appears in a selectable field and nothing is marked sent.
- Editing a sent annotation makes it unsent again; opening and cancelling keeps it sent.
- After a hot update, and after a reload, sent annotations on items that now render differently are removed with "Cleared N sent pins on updated items" and Undo. Unsent annotations, and sent ones on unchanged items (including other items from the same source file), stay. A theme switch never clears anything.
- A sent new-version annotation stays while its item changes, and is removed with "Cleared N sent pins: new version added" and Undo once a new item with `from` set to its item appears. Removing such an item clears nothing.
- Clear removes every annotation and offers Undo.
- Alt-click still copies a single reference, in and out of annotate mode, and places no pin.

## Sending to the agent

Run `scripts/listen.sh <canvas-url>` in a terminal as the agent would.

- While it waits, the copy button reads "Send N to agent". Pressing it makes `listen.sh` print the same message Copy writes, plus a line telling the agent to listen again, and exit. The pins turn into sent pins and "Sent N to the agent" shows.
- An open note has one button beside the delete button. While an agent listens, it sends everything that waits and says one word: "Send" (or "Send N" when more than one waits), and a screen reader hears the same name as the bar at the top ("Send N to agent"). With no listener it says "Done" and only keeps the note and closes it, like Enter; the bar at the top ("Copy N for agent") is the one place that copies, and it counts a note as soon as it has words, before Done. A reopened sent note also says Done. Enter keeps the note and closes it, so the next one can be placed; ⌘/Ctrl + Enter sends, or copies everything with no listener; Shift + Enter breaks the line; and Enter that confirms an IME composition does none of these. With nothing typed the button is off and still readable: grey words on a grey ground, not white on a pale tint.
- An open note has no title: the pin, or the outline of an area or a whole item, shows what it is on. Its top edge, marked with a short bar, is a handle across the whole width: drag it to see what is under the note. The field keeps its words and focus. The move is for this time only: opened again, the note is at its pin.
- Nothing the person reads says "annotation": the delete button's tip is "Delete", a screen reader hears the card as "Ask agent about <reference>" and a pin as "For the agent, N: …", "Sent to the agent: …", or "Queued for the agent: …". Only the message the agent reads keeps the heading "Annotations from the design canvas (N):".
- A pin stays on its element when its item is resized, from any edge, during the drag and after it: a pin on a centered dialog moves with the dialog, and a pin near the end of a line of text keeps its place in the text. The same in a `viewport` item, a moment after the new size is saved. A whole-item note keeps covering the whole item. A drawn area, and a pin whose element's words have changed since, stay at their point. After a reload the pins are where they were left.
- White words on a filled control read at 4.5:1 or better on the default colors: the send button, a numbered pin, an unread answered pin's head, and the "Picked" tab use the darkened fill color, not the focus color.
- Each entry in a sent or copied message ends with `(note <id>)`. `scripts/reply.sh <canvas-url> <id> "<text>"` puts the text on that note: the pin becomes one shape, a filled head with the answer's first line after it ("Agent: …", cut with an ellipsis when long, up to 300 px), and a notice says the agent replied, with Show, which brings the note's item into view without changing the zoom. No card opens by itself. Near the canvas's right edge the words run to the left of the head, which stays on its point. Words with quotes, line breaks, and Japanese or Chinese arrive as written.
- Opening an answered note shows a thread, each turn with "You" or "Agent" above it, in plain text: the person's turns at the edge with no mark, and the agent's turns set in by a rule. A long thread shows whole: the note grows, and nothing inside it scrolls. The field below is empty, says "Reply to the agent", and there is no new-version checkbox while the agent's reply is the last turn. The pin then turns quiet (outlined, no words) and stays read after Escape and after a reload.
- Typing an answer and keeping or sending it keeps the same pin: the turns so far stay above, the answer becomes the note's words, and the message's entry lists them under "Earlier on this note:" with the answer after "Now:". The agent's next reply adds a turn; nothing is doubled after a reload.
- An answered note does not clear when its item changes; a sent note with no answer still does. An old answer never attaches to newer words. Deleting the note removes the whole thread.
- An answer for a note that is no longer on the canvas shows once as a notice and is not repeated when the page opens again. An answer sent while the page is reloading is on its pin after the reload. A page in the browser cannot post a reply (403), and an id that is not a note id is refused (400).
- Without a listener, after a listener is stopped, or on a canvas whose vite config lacks `agentChannel()`, the button reads "Copy N for agent" and copies. `listen.sh` on such a canvas says it cannot send, and on a stopped dev server says it is not running.
- After a delivery, until `listen.sh` runs again, Send queues: the pins show a clock, "Queued N. The agent gets them when it's free." shows, and the next `listen.sh` receives them at once as one message, numbered from 1. Editing a queued annotation makes it unsent.
- Stopping `listen.sh` or the dev server while annotations are queued turns them back into numbered unsent ones with "The agent stopped listening, so N weren't sent"; so does a reload while no agent listens. 15 minutes without a new listener after a delivery or timeout count as stopped.
- After a delivery, each item and section with an annotation in it shows an "Agent working" caption above its top-right corner (plain grey text, no badge or animation, one size on screen at any zoom), and sent note, section, and move changes show it in place of their "Sent" tag, until `listen.sh` runs again; then the tags go and "The agent finished" shows. No bar shows at the top unless something is still to apply. A quiet timeout and the `listen.sh` that follows show neither.
- A listener with `keep=1` (the Codex listener) keeps the "Agent working" tags across its reconnects. `POST /__design-canvas/agent/done` without `Origin` ends it with "The agent finished"; with an `Origin` header it gets 403. Stopping the listener or 15 minutes without done end it silently.
- A second `listen.sh` replaces the first, which prints that another session is listening and exits.
- Stopping the dev server switches the button back to Copy; `listen.sh` reconnects by itself after a dev server restart.
- Requests to `/__design-canvas/agent/listen` with an `Origin` or `Sec-Fetch-Site` header, and sends to `/__design-canvas/agent/send` from another origin or host, get 403.
- Annotations survive reload and hot reload, including one being typed; annotations on removed items are dropped. Corrupt saved annotations fall back to none without errors.

## Trash

- Moving an item to the trash hides it at once with "Moved <name> to the trash" and Undo, keeps focus on the canvas, and leaves other items in place. Its unsent annotations are hidden, not copied, and not cleared; they return with it.
- The trash button shows the count. It opens a large dialog over the dimmed canvas: every item as a card with a live thumbnail (a `viewport` item at its own width, scaled down to fit, never up) and its name, most recently trashed first, under Today, Yesterday, and Earlier, each with the time (today and yesterday) or the date it went in. An item trashed in another browser, or before the canvas kept times, has no time and stays in its place in the order. Thumbnails take no clicks, focus, or Alt-picks.
- Thumbnails draw two at a time from the top, and only once scrolled near view: in a canvas of slow `viewport` items the first cards show well before the last, instead of all at once after a wait.
- A click on a card (or Enter on it) opens it large over the cards, as its own page in a frame, the same as Open in new tab: it takes clicks and typing, its links and router calls stay in it, and nothing done in it changes the canvas. Each card opens fresh. It shows its place ("3 / 7"), Previous and Next (also ← and →; dimmed at the ends, where they do nothing and keep focus; not shown for a trash of one), and Put back. The cards stay drawn underneath and stop loading meanwhile, so the opened one loads first. Back, or Escape, returns to the cards as they were, scrolled to the card last looked at, which is marked and focused. A single click never puts an item back. While focus is inside the opened item, keys are the item's own; Escape, ←, and → work again after a press outside it.
- Opening the trash focuses the first card; Tab stays inside the dialog. Escape closes it (from an open card, it first goes back) and returns focus to the button, as does a press on the dimmed canvas. After a press on the dialog's empty space, Escape still works. At 600 px the cards fit the window.
- Set aside, beside Put back, takes the item out of the trash into the "Set aside" section a pick fills: at the right end of its row, which grows to hold it, or, with no such section yet, a new one below everything. The registry gets the item's place without `trashed`, and the section, in one write besides the trash's own. The trash stays open on the next item (the one before it, at the end) and closes when it has nothing left; the view does not move.
- Put back closes the trash and puts the item back where it was, or beside its row if that spot is now taken, then brings it into view and to the front. The trash button goes away when the trash is empty.
- Show all and links to `#<item-id>` ignore trashed items. The trash survives reload; ids no longer in the registry drop out.
- Moving an item to the trash writes `trashed: true` into its registry entry, and putting it back removes it, leaving the entry as it was. A trash saved only in the browser, by an older shell, is written on the next load.
- Changing `trashed` in source moves that item into or out of the trash on every open canvas. A new item with `trashed: true` starts in the trash.
- Opening the canvas never changes `trashed` in the registry, even in a tab or browser whose saved trash differs: it shows the registry's trash. Only moving an item into or out of the trash writes it.
- An item whose `trashed` cannot be written (its entry is not found exactly once) stays in the trash in this browser, across reloads.

## New versions

- A new registry item with `from` set appears right of that item, else below it, else at the nearer end of that item's row or column; no other item moves. The camera shows both, fully above the toolbar and the notice that announces the new version.

## Viewport items

- A responsive component shows its narrow layout in a narrow `viewport` item while the window is wide.
- `frame.html?item=<id>` alone renders the item at its size without chrome, centered in a larger tab and scrollable in a smaller one; an unknown id shows a short message.
- Opened alone in a wide tab, a `viewport` item still shows its narrow layout.

## Shared file

Run `scripts/share.sh` on a canvas with a `viewport` item, and open the file from disk in Chrome and Safari, and inside another page the way a preview pane or an embed shows it (an iframe whose `src` is a blob of the file, or whose `srcdoc` is the file).

- Every item renders and works, with no console errors. Ask agent, its A shortcut, its help line, and Share are gone; Alt-click still copies a reference.
- An image an item loads from the app's public folder by root path shows; a root path with no file there is named by share.sh.
- Wheel, pinch, Space-drag, and Alt-click over a `viewport` item reach the canvas, as on the dev server.
- The open-alone icon opens the same file with `?item=<id>`, which shows that item alone. Inside another page the file has no address to open, so the icon is not there.
- Inside another page, every `viewport` frame renders, and wheel over one still pans the canvas.
- Items in the trash are not in a file made with the Share button at all; a file made with `share.sh` keeps them out of sight.
- Reload keeps the layout. A newer shared file of the same canvas opens as it was shared, not with the layout saved in an older one.
- On the dev server, the Share icon button (tooltip "Share as an HTML file") opens a panel under it, "Share as a file", with focus on Download. A canvas with titled sections offers "Whole canvas · N items" or each section (not set-aside frames), in reading order; a canvas without sections shows no choice. A line says the file opens in any browser and goes into Figma or Notion, and Download sits under it, the width of the panel. What is inside a section is what has its centre inside the frame, where the person has it now; set-aside frames go with whatever they sit in.
- Download builds the file without the trash and without what lies outside the chosen section (their words and code are not in the file), shows "Preparing the file…" meanwhile, and downloads `<canvas title> <date>.html` (or `<canvas title> · <section> <date>.html`) without reloading the page. The panel closes, focus returns to Share, and the notice at the top centre says "Downloaded …", with any file it could not include. Escape, the close button, or a press outside closes the panel and returns focus to Share; opening the trash closes it too.

## Layout

- Reload keeps camera and positions; removed ids are ignored.
- A new registry item appears at its source position when that spot is free. When it overlaps the saved layout, it moves to free space beside its row. Either way the camera stays where it is (a new version too) and existing items stay put. The new item shows a green "New" tag above its top-right corner until it has been at least half on screen, at least 120 px wide, for 2 s while the page is in front; then the tag goes. An item whose in-place note cleared after the agent's change shows "Updated" the same way. The tags survive a reload until seen, and the next reload treats the item as known.
- Corrupt or oversized saved data, or blocked storage, falls back to defaults without errors.
- The console shows no errors or warnings from the shell.

## Tools, notes, and sections

- The tools bar has Interact, Move canvas, Inspect (with a design system), Ask agent, Note, and Section; exactly one is pressed. Interact is the default: in it, the prototypes respond to clicks and typing as in the product. Escape or V returns to Interact from any of them, A toggles Ask agent, N and S pick the Note and Section tools, none of these while typing in a field.
- Note and Section work without Ask agent mode. While one is armed the cursor is its own everywhere (see Cursors) and every press, also over items, notes, and sections, goes to the canvas; it puts itself down after one use. Picking Annotate or Select puts it down too.
- Note: one click places a sticker with its field focused, showing the "Write a note" placeholder and no tag; typing works at once. Escape or a press elsewhere keeps the text. An empty note stays as a draft: placeholder, no state, not counted or sent, kept in browser storage; its discard button removes it. Once it has text it is appended to `notes` in `registry.tsx` with an id made from its text, shows no tag while it saves, and its ghost leaves when the registry has one more note.
- Section: a drag draws the outline, then a folder with the "Section title" placeholder focused on the tab; Enter keeps it. Left empty it stays as a draft, like a note; with a title it is appended to `sections` the same way. With no dev server a new note or section stays "Not applied yet" and goes to the agent. A drag under 80×60 canvas units makes nothing and keeps the tool.
- A section looks like a folder: its title is on a raised tab joined to the frame, in tints of the canvas colors in both themes. A note is a sticker with a cut, folded bottom-right corner in fixed colors in both themes.
- A section's title tab keeps one size on screen (about 14 px text) at 10%, 50%, 100%, and 300% zoom, and stays joined to the frame's top-left corner. It is at most 70% of the frame's width, but never narrower than 160 px on screen, so a small frame or a zoomed-out canvas still shows a readable, truncated title that may reach past the frame.
- In Select, dragging a note, or a section's tab, moves it at every zoom; a press that never travels stays a click and selects it. A click on empty space inside a section selects it too (the innermost one where sections overlap); a drag there pans and keeps the selection, and a click on empty canvas outside every section lets go of it. A note or a section resizes from its border like an item, selected or not: over any edge or corner the cursor turns into a resize cursor (nothing else changes), the grab zone is about 10 px on screen at any zoom, an edge changes only its side and a corner both, the opposite side stays put, and it stops at a minimum size. A click on the border selects it. A selected one also shows four corner grips, which resize it the same way. A section's tab wins over the top edge under it. A note or section stays selected after its move or resize is saved. A section inside another is drawn above it, whatever their order in the registry. A section carries the items, the notes, and the sections wholly inside it: dragging the section, or nudging it with arrow keys, moves them by the same amount, and dragging it back restores them with no change left. A registry note carried this way is its own change, listed separately. Arrow keys move the focused one (Shift for larger steps), Enter or a double-click edits it.
- While the agent handles a message sent from the canvas, a beam rides each item's preview and each section's frame its annotations are on: a short comet of colour going round on a 2 px line over a soft glow, clearly visible on a light canvas, one size on screen at any zoom, in CSS only. The beam lays over the box: the preview never reloads or loses state. An item's beam fades out once its change arrives and its note clears; all fade when the agent finishes or stops listening. Copied notes, queued notes, and older sent notes not in that message show nothing. Under reduced motion the beam is a still gradient line. Nothing is locked: the items can still be moved, annotated, and used, and a new version still lands beside its original where it is now.
- While an item or a note is dragged, the section it will be in once dropped is outlined and tinted: the one holding its centre, the rule a section's moves and notes use. An edge over a section is not enough, an item already inside stays outlined as it moves within it, and the outline goes when the drag ends or the centre leaves. It stays one width on screen at any zoom. Dragging a section outlines nothing.
- A registry update, from a save or from the agent, never cancels a drag in progress (an annotation area, a move): the `items`, `notes`, and `sections` arrays keep their identity while their entries are equal.
- Delete or Backspace on a selected note or section removes its entry from `registry.tsx`, hides it at once, and shows "Note deleted" or "Section deleted" with Undo, which puts the entry back with its id, text, position, and size. A section's items and notes stay. The keys do nothing while a field has them (a note being edited, an annotation), in a shared file, or with no dev server, where "Couldn't delete it" shows and the entry comes back.
- The caption under an item's name (its `note`) is edited with a double-click: the text becomes a one-line field, Enter or leaving it saves into `registry.tsx` (the new text shows at once), Escape cancels, an empty or unchanged caption saves nothing. The canvas's keys (A, N, S, Delete) stay out of the field. A shared file cannot edit captions.
- When an automatic save cannot go through, a notice says why: "Restart this canvas's dev server" when the server does not answer (or does not know the route), or that the entry could not be saved, with a hint that an entry generated by code (a computed id or number) must be written out by hand, when its registry entry has no plain id or a computed value. The change then waits for the agent as "Not applied yet".
- ⌘/Ctrl + Z undoes the last saved change from anywhere on the canvas, including with focus on an item's grip, and not inside a text field or a preview: a moved item (and its place in the browser), a resize, an edit, a caption, a new note (removed again), a deleted note or section (put back). Each press undoes one saved change, newest first; "Nothing to undo" shows when there is none, and an undo is not itself saved as new history. It does not survive a reload. The help list mentions it, and in a shared file it is gone.
- After typing in a preview's field, dragging a section, a note, or an item (or resizing one) takes keyboard focus back to the canvas, so the next ⌘/Ctrl + Z undoes that drag. A section dragged with items and notes inside is one change: one ⌘/Ctrl + Z puts the frame and everything inside back. ⌘/Ctrl + Z pressed right after a drag, before its save, undoes that drag, with no ghost left behind.
- A pause in the middle of a drag (move or resize, of an item, a note, or a section) saves nothing: the note or section keeps following the pointer, and one save follows the release.
- A save never reloads the page (a marker set on `window` survives it), also with a frame page open or `viewport` items on the canvas. In an app with Tailwind, a save also keeps the canvas mounted and every `viewport` frame loaded (markers set on the canvas DOM and inside each frame survive it), and the dev server logs an update of `main.tsx` and `frame.tsx` only, with no stylesheet. Moving, resizing, or editing a note or section from the registry saves it into `registry.tsx` after a short pause: its ghost shows no tag and leaves once the registry matches. Putting it back where it began removes the ghost. With no dev server the change falls back to "Not applied yet" and the agent message.
- Ghosts are dashed and tagged "Not applied yet", turn "Sent · waiting for the agent" or "Queued" after sending, stay across a reload, and a ghost section stays behind its items. Moving a sent ghost makes it a new request.
- The button in the changes bar counts notes, sections, and annotations together and says "Send N to agent" with a listening agent, "Copy N for agent" without. Leaving annotate mode with changes waiting shows "N changes aren't applied yet" with a button that applies or copies them.
- The message lists the changes in words (add, move, resize, text or title) and, for a section, the items inside it with their current positions.
- Adding a note or section to the registry replaces its sent ghost; a change's ghost leaves once the entry matches its text, position, and size.
- Discard all (the eraser) drops annotations, ghosts, and moved items' new positions in one step, with Undo for all of it; its toast counts what the Send button counted.
- Registry notes and sections draw in the shared file, sections behind items, with no tools, grips, or ghosts. Fit all includes them.
- A section shows the grip an item's name has on its tab, and its frame lights up while the tab is hovered; pulling the tab moves it. A drag on a section's border resizes the frame alone and never moves it or the items inside; only the tab moves a section.

## Note formatting

- A note shows `**words**` in bold and lines that start with `- `, `* `, or `• ` as a bulleted list, and with `1. ` or `1) ` as a numbered list that starts at the number written. Everything else shows as typed, with its line breaks and blank lines. Stars with a space inside them (`2 ** 3 ** 4`) stay as typed. ## Selection

- On the canvas page with the default tool, a drag that starts on empty canvas draws a box, and everything the box touches is selected as it is drawn: items, notes, and reference images. A section is selected only when the box holds all of its frame, so a box drawn inside a section selects what is in it. The camera does not move. Shift + drag adds to what was selected. A press on empty canvas that never travels is the click it always was: it selects the section under it, or lets go of the selection.
- A click on an item's name selects that item alone; it shows the outline a selected note shows, around its preview. Shift + click on an item's name, a note, a section's tab, or an image adds it to the selection, or takes it out, and starts no move.
- With several selected, each shows its outline and none shows resize grips. Dragging any one of them moves them all by the same amount; a selected section also carries what is inside it, and nothing moves twice. The dragged item still snaps to the items that are not moving. Dragging something that is not selected selects it alone and moves only it.
- The whole move is one write to `registry.tsx` and one ⌘/Ctrl + Z. The selection is kept once the move is saved, and after the undo.
- Delete or Backspace removes the selection in one step: items go to the trash, notes, sections, and images leave the registry, and one notice ("Removed 3 from the canvas") has one Undo that brings all of it back. A section's contents stay unless they were selected too.
- Escape, a click on empty canvas, or a press anywhere that is not selectable (a preview, a bar) lets go of the selection. Shift + 2 fits the view to it.
- None of it in a shared file, where nothing can move, or with Ask agent chosen, where a click on a name is a note. Not verified by touch: one finger on empty canvas pans.

## Reference images

- Pasting a copied picture (⌘/Ctrl + V) puts it on the canvas at the pointer, or in the middle of the view when the pointer is off the canvas. Dropping image files from the desktop puts them where they are dropped, several a step apart. It shows at once, dimmed, and at full strength once it is saved. Not while typing in a field or with focus in a preview, where the paste belongs to the app shown.
- The file is written into the canvas's `images/` folder as `image-N` with the extension of the format it was stored in, and `registry.tsx` gets an entry in `images` (`{ id, file, x, y, width, height }`). A registry made before images existed gets the array added above `registry`. A new image is shown at most 640 px on its longest side.

A GIF goes as it is, so one that moves keeps moving. In a browser that cannot write WebP (Safari) the picture is stored as JPEG, or as PNG when it has see-through parts. Not verified in Safari. A file the browser cannot read gives a notice and adds nothing.
- An image moves by a drag and resizes from a corner when it alone is selected; it keeps its proportions, and the size shows by the pointer. Moves and resizes are saved like an item's, and ⌘/Ctrl + Z puts them back. It is selected, moved with a selection, carried by a section whose frame holds its centre, and deleted like a note. Deleting it takes its entry out and leaves the file, so Undo can list it again.
- Images draw above sections and under items. Fit all takes them in.
- The shared file holds each picture inside it and shows it; it cannot be moved there. Sharing one section still puts every image in the file.
- With no dev server the picture is not added, and a notice says why.
- Not built: asking the agent about an image with a pin, cropping, and keyboard moves.

## Moved items

- Dragging an item saves its position to `registry.tsx` after a short pause, with no tag: a save that goes through says nothing. Only a save still on its way after about 2.5 seconds shows "Saving…" (on an item, a note, or a section), until the registry has it. A browser test holds a write open to check this; by hand it needs a slow or stopped write. With no dev server it shows "Not applied yet" and a ↺ button, and the changes bar counts it. A shared file shows neither.
- A save puts the new `registry.tsx` in place in one step (written beside it as `.registry.tsx.saving`, then renamed over it), so the file is never empty or half written while the page hot-updates. Many saves in a row leave no console error such as "does not provide an export named 'notes'", no full reload, and no `.saving` file. A registry made read-only is still refused.
- A change written to `registry.tsx` while a save is on its way (the agent adds an item while an item is dragged) stays, and the save lands on top of it. A save that cannot find its item in the changed file is treated as one the registry refused. Without a lock a write in the last moment before the swap can still be lost, so check this with many saves, not one.
- The message lists "Move “Name” (id x) to x,y". After sending, the tag says "Sent · waiting for the agent" ("Queued" while the agent is busy); moving the item again makes it new again.
- ↺ puts the item back where the registry has it and ends the change. An item whose registry position changes to match loses its tag on its own.
- Items inside a section that is waiting for the agent are one change with it: no tag, not counted twice.
- Notes and items inside a section that moves are saved with it, in the same write, and show no tag of their own.
- An empty draft note or section is never saved; it moves with its section and is discarded with its ✕ button.
- Opening a canvas whose registry has a generated item (a computed id, `x`/`y`/`width`/`height`, or a `...spread`) shows one notice naming up to three such items, telling the agent to write them out by hand. A canvas with every entry written out shows none. With no dev server or an older one, nothing shows.
- When GitHub's `VERSION` is higher than the canvas's own (`canvas/VERSION`, written by install.sh and update.sh), opening the canvas shows one notice, "Version N of this canvas is out", with "Ask the agent to update". With an agent listening the button sends the request; otherwise it copies it. The same version is not offered again in that browser. Offline, with `DESIGN_CANVAS_NO_UPDATE_CHECK=1`, in a shared file, or with an older dev server, nothing shows.
- On a canvas of 20+ plain (non-`viewport`) items whose CSS has `:has()` rules, panning and zooming stay smooth: in a Chrome performance trace, frames during a pan or zoom show no style recalculation of the whole page (`UpdateLayoutTree` over a few hundred elements). The zoom label still updates while zooming.

## Inspect (prototype)

Needs a canvas whose `design-system.ts` names a real design system. The checks below used Aegis 3.4 (with the full config in the kit's `design-system.ts`), Radix Themes 3.3 (`name`, `components`, `package`, and `propTypes` from its prop definitions), MUI 7.3 (with `createTheme({ cssVariables: true })`, and without it plus `tokens` from the theme), Chakra UI 3.38, and shadcn/ui on Tailwind 4.3 (the last three with `name`, `components`, and `package` only, none for shadcn/ui).

- MUI: the breadcrumb names components (`Card › CardContent › Stack › Button`), not their `div`s. Button lists `color`, `size`, `variant`, and its on/off props with MUI's defaults; Typography lists `variant` `h1`…`overline`, and nothing from the theme. Only `--mui-*` properties are tokens. A contained button reads Fill `primary-main` and Text `primary-contrastText`; padding 16px reads `spacing 2`, 6px stays outside the system. Without `cssVariables`, with `tokens` from the theme, the same rows read by the theme's paths (`primary.main`, `primary.contrastText`, `shape.borderRadius`, `spacing 2`), a pick shows its value, and the note names the path. Where several tokens share a value, the one in the family of the element's fill wins (`primary.contrastText` on `primary.main`).
- Chakra UI: the breadcrumb says `Card.Root › Card.Body › Stack › Button`. Button lists `size` 2xl…2xs and `variant` solid…plain from its recipe, with defaults; Button, Badge, and Heading offer `colorPalette` gray…pink (default gray), and a pick recolors the component at once; Stack, which has no recipe, does not. Tokens go by their short names (`gray-900`, `l2`, `4xl`, `spacing-4`); Fill offers `bg-*` and every palette color.
- shadcn/ui: Button lists `variant` (default…link) and `size` (default…icon-lg) from its `cva`, with defaults; CardFooter, which has none, says it has default props. Text offers every `*-foreground`, Fill `primary`, `secondary`, `muted`, `accent`, `card`, and the rest; Padding reads Tailwind steps (`spacing 4`); Radius offers `xs` to `4xl` and reads `rounded-md` as `md`; Type offers `xs` to `9xl`; Fill and Text offer Tailwind's whole palette (`color-red-500`) after the app's own colors, also shades no class uses: a pick of one shows its value and the note names it. No `--tw-*` property appears, and `--color-primary: var(--primary)` is not listed beside `primary`.
- Without settings, tokens are found by value and name: on Radix, Padding and Gap list `space-1` to `space-9`, Radius `radius-*`, Type `font-size-1` to `font-size-9` (no attribute), a color row every color; no component's own tokens (`inset-*`, `button.column.gap`) join a kind whose system has names that start with it.

- The Inspect tool (I) shows only with a design system configured, and never in a shared file. A click in a preview selects the design-system component the app placed (a click on Button's label selects Button); a double-click selects exactly what is under the pointer. A click on a link selects it and follows nothing: the item stays on its screen. Arrow Up and Down move between levels; Escape lets go of the element, then puts the tool down.
- As in a browser's element picker, the box marks what is being chosen, never what is chosen: it follows the element under the pointer in a preview, shows a breadcrumb level while hovered, and shows a new selection made by Arrow Up or Down, a double-click, or a breadcrumb press. It goes once the pointer moves anywhere else in the panel or its lists, and while any value is on show (also chosen by keys), so no outline sits on the element while a change is previewed or judged.
- The breadcrumb names components and readable classes (`Card › CardBody › div.actions › Button`), and each level selects.
- A component lists its design props with the values its types allow and its default marked. Changing one re-renders the component at once; the picker shows the new value. Its own styles are read-only and nothing in them is marked.
- An element the app built shows padding, gap, margin, radius, fill, and (with text) type and color as pickers of tokens only. A value outside the system is marked, with the nearest tokens. Picking a token shows it on the element at once.
- A picker shows each option on the element while the pointer is over it, and puts back what was there when the pointer leaves the list, on Escape, on a press outside, or on a click on the current value, however many options it passed over; none of that makes a note. A click or Enter picks the option. Arrow Up and Down move through the options and show each one; scrolling the list never moves the option under a still pointer. A list longer than 12 has a search field, and colors show swatches and go by the name after their kind (`accent.blue.subtle`). While a picker is open the panel does not reflow under it.
- A list opens over its field with the current value on the field's line, its text where the field's text was, so the pointer that opened it rests on it. Opening shows nothing new until the pointer moves; one row up or down shows the next value. A second click there closes the list and is not a change. Near the top or bottom bar the list scrolls to keep the value on its line, and stays between the bars at any window height. Sizes go biggest first: lengths by value (a value outside the system between its nearest tokens, 0 last), props whose values all name a size (`xSmall`…`xLarge`, `1`…`4`, `none`…`full`) by size, and typography by role, biggest first, each bold right after its regular. Other props keep the library's order; colors keep theirs.
- Hovering or focusing a breadcrumb level outlines that element on the canvas; leaving takes the outline away.
- A design-system component's own styles are folded and locked at first. Unfolded and locked, they are read-only chips; the lock unlocks them to pickers (tokens only, nothing marked as outside the system). A change there shows on the component and its note line says "(overrides Button)", with a line asking to apply overrides to this instance only and leave the design system as it is. The fold and the lock stay as set while the tool is on; a section with a change stays open.
- Each changed element gets one numbered note: one line per change, then a line asking for the named tokens and props, this element only. "Reset to …" takes one change back and its line out; a note left empty goes. Discard takes every change off the elements; Undo after it brings them back.
- The panel's left edge resizes it (drag, or Arrow Left and Right on the focused edge), between 240 and 560 px, and the width is kept for the next time. The panel is as tall as its content, down to just above the view controls; opening it moves no bar. The bars and their hints and menus (help, Canvas settings) draw above the panel and its lists, and a list stops short of the bars. Hints on the panel's own buttons open under the button and stay inside the panel.
- With an element selected, the canvas pans and zooms as smoothly as with no selection: in a performance trace, panning shows no repeated style reads, and the panel reads again only when the preview changes. Selecting an element takes well under 100 ms on the Aegis lab canvas.
- Changing a value moves nothing in the panel: no bar or line appears or goes, and no row changes height. A changed value carries a mark on its left; its list starts with "Reset to <what the code had>", which shows the original while hovered and puts it back when picked. On/off switches have a ↺ instead. The props and styles headings each show a reset icon while that section has changes: it puts back every change in the section (other elements' changes stay), with a notice that offers Undo. Changes in a note already sent are not counted or reset.
- A value outside the system is amber, says why on hover, and its list marks the nearest tokens "nearest"; it has no line of its own under the row.
- The panel reads as a design tool's inspector: a label column and fields that fill the rest, one line and one height each, so values line up. A field shows a token by its name after its kind (`small` in Padding, `accent.blue.subtle` in Text) and the whole name on hover; the note for the agent says the whole name. Two values share a row without arrows; four go two by two. A changed row has a dot before its label. On and off are switches. A component's read-only values are the same fields without a surface. Lists open with a short fade, not under reduced motion.
- After a reload, waiting notes stay but their changes no longer show on the elements (prototype shortcut).
